Membrane transport

How cells move ions and small molecules across a bilayer that is, by design, almost impermeable to them.

Passive transport

Solutes move down their electrochemical gradient without an energy input. Small nonpolar molecules diffuse straight through the bilayer; ions and polar molecules need a protein path.

  • Channels form gated pores and are fast, up to 10⁸ ions per second.
  • Carriers bind the solute and change shape. They are slower and can saturate.

Active transport

The Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP. That makes it electrogenic: it contributes directly to the resting membrane potential.

5 / 14
Question

Why does the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase count as electrogenic?

Each cycle moves three positive charges out and only two in, so there is a net outward current that adds directly to the membrane potential.
